# The small-constituent screen

The numerical data in `EPPAIII/Primitive/SmallConstituentTable.lean` records the 168 source rows below order 461 in Brouwer and Van Maldeghem, *Strongly Regular Graphs*, Table 11.8. Those rows give 158 distinct parameter tuples. The authors' preprint puts these rows on pages 365–367. The order-5 Paley graph is a pentagon, so its tuple is `(5,2,0,1)`. The preprint prints 1 in its third column, which fails the strongly regular parameter equation. Correcting that entry does not affect the screen. [Source, §11.6](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~aeb/math/srg/rk3/srgw.pdf#page=381)

`calculatedSmallConstituentHits_eq` computes both orientations and compares the result with the 24 distinct profile, constituent position, and host parameter hits in R17. Splitting the repeated profiles for the two Taylor doubles and the two independent blowups gives the 30 graph-labelled entries in the manuscript. The equal-degree complement of `T(7)` has a different adjacent common-neighbor count and is explicitly considered. The calculation is a kernel proof using `decide`.

`constituentGraph_profile` derives the rooted profile from the actual host adjacency and its strongly regular parameters. The first constituent counts common neighbors of the root and a neighbor. In the second constituent, each vertex's neighbors split into the common neighbors of it and the root, and the neighbors inside the second constituent. For a connected noncomplete strongly regular graph, the nonedge common-neighbor count is positive. This also applies to the complement of a primitive rank-three graph. These facts justify the order bound 460 on the host for either constituent.

`SmallRankThreeParameterCoverage` expresses the external assertion that the oriented table includes the parameters of every primitive rank-three host of order at most 460. `SmallRankThreeModelIdentification` expresses the external graph isomorphism assertions for the 21 distinct oriented host parameter tuples that survive the screen. These assertions refer to concrete models. The group classification supports both inputs, and the Lean computation checks the filter after these inputs are supplied. The nine actual induced-constituent isomorphism occurrences reported in R17 are separate from the 24 numerical hits. The calculations here do not certify the nine occurrences.

The degree-5 folded 5-cube is the Clebsch orientation of R17. Brouwer and Van Maldeghem use the name Clebsch for its complement in §10.7. The degree-16 Schläfli model uses the double-six construction of §10.10 with the 15 two-subsets of six points. Its complement is the order-27 screen hit. [Source, Chapter 10](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~aeb/math/srg/rk3/srgw.pdf#page=267)
