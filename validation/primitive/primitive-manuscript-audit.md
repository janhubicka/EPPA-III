For the first counterexample, use the quadratic residues modulo 29 as the local vertex set and parameterize them by \(4^j\) for \(j\in\mathbb Z/14\). The nonneighbors of 1 have 3 common neighbors with 1 at \(j=2,12\), 4 at \(j=7\), and 2 at \(j=1,5,9,13\). Multiplication by a quadratic residue preserves the local graph. Thus the invariant graph whose edges are nonadjacent pairs with exactly three common neighbors is two disjoint 7-cycles, with automorphism group order \(2\cdot14^2\). The local automorphism group has order dividing this number, so it cannot have a section \(S_3\). Every induced graph on six or more vertices contains a clique or independent set of size three. Therefore no such induced graph can have this subconstituent as its EPPA witness. Multiplication by a nonsquare identifies the other subconstituent with its complement. The varying nonedge codegrees, or the corresponding varying edge codegrees in the complement, also rule out rank 3.

The formerly unconditional half-order sentence in the remark at line 3393 already has the non-homogeneity qualifier in R17. For a homogeneous generalized cover such as \(2K_5\), \(\kappa=10\), while half its order is 5.

## 3. Proof replacements for review

### Factorial bound on the actual selected subgraph

The local-section corollary applies to subsets of \(G_\alpha\). It does not imply that \(\operatorname{Aut}(H_\alpha)\) is a section of the host vertex stabilizer. Thus an intrinsic wreath-product subgroup of an arbitrary cover is insufficient.

The inductive classification of the **pair** shows that the selected graph, up to complement, is a union of cliques, possibly unequal. If it has \(a\) components and largest component size \(b\), then \(ab\ge |G_\alpha|\ge |H_\alpha|/2\). A largest clique and a transversal of the components give the necessary symmetric section via the local-section corollary. The resulting divisor is \(\lfloor\sqrt{|H_\alpha|/2}\rfloor!\), for the selected index \(\alpha\). A use of a full wreath-product order requires establishing the equal-clique structure of the **selected** graph at that site. [Patch](../patches/02-sections-7-8.patch)

### Grassmann and bilinear forms graphs

For Grassmann graphs, the displayed factorial estimate fails outside the listed exceptions. At \((n,q)=(7,2)\) it compares \(6!\) with \(2^{39}\), and \(6!\) is smaller even than the actual point stabilizer of order \(61436067840\). The semilinear argument for the clique-size bound is also missing a justification. The bilinear proof needs the selected local graph, and the stabilizer formula printed there evaluates to 1008 at \((m,q)=(3,2)\), whereas the proof claims 2016.

The proposed replacement uses adjacency and elementary counting. For a connected \(d\)-regular graph of order \(N\) with \(d<N/2-1\), the cover degree equation excludes an ordinary generalized cover. Its complement can be a cover of \(sK_t\) only if the original graph contains an independent set of order \(t\ge N/2-d\). For Grassmann first and second subconstituents, the bounds are respectively \(q+1\), by intersections with the fixed line, and \((q^n-1)/(q^2-1)\), by disjoint nonzero vectors. For rank-one and rank-two bilinear subconstituents, the bounds are \(q+1\), by column spaces, and \(q^m\), by first rows. The only surviving case is a possible complement cover of \(3K_7\) in the second constituent of \(H_2(2,3)\). It would give \(K_{7,14}\) in a host whose nonadjacent vertices have six common neighbors. The patched proofs supply the connectivity arguments and uniform inequalities. [Review patch](../alternatives/03-grassmann-bilinear-review.patch)

### The missing unitary graph on 45 vertices

The old cross-reference misses \(U_4(2)\), since the polar lemma assumes \(q>2\). For the standard Hermitian form on \(\mathbb F_4^4\), choose \(v=(0,0,1,1)\). The 12 local points are \((1,a,b,b)\) with \(a\ne0\), forming three \(K_4\)'s indexed by \(a\). Any selection of seven local points contains either independent triples with different common-neighbor counts, or two copies of \(2K_2\) for which the union of the cross-pair common-neighbor sets induces different edge counts. Both quantities are invariant under automorphisms fixing \(v\). The patch gives the coordinate argument and the checker verifies all 792 selections, split into 768 and 24 respectively.

It follows that the second selected intersection has at least \(23-1-6=16\) vertices. Its host has order 32 and degree 9, with complement degree 22. Neither is an equal-clique union. The only possible generalized cover occurs in the complement and has \(s=2,t=8,X=\overline M,Y=1\). This puts \(K_{8,8}\) inside the actual second constituent, contradicting the host common-neighbor count \(\mu=3\). The exact graph model is given by the Hermitian form. The certificate uses no assumed automorphism group or EPPA result. Lean also checks the normalized model order, the exact local vertex list and the six disjoint-edge counts by kernel reduction.
