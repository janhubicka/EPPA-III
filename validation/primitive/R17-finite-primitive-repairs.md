# EPPA III R17 finite primitive repairs

The patch applies to `main-new(20261116-151839)-markers-r17.tex`, version 1. The latexdiff shows just the changed passages. Patch applicability was checked against the exact materialized source. The frozen source was used read-only.

## Incorrect assertions and counterexamples

- **The 36-vertex orthogonal graph.** Brouwer–Van Maldeghem, §3.1.4, identify `NO_3^+(q)` with `T(q+1)`. Thus the active sentence dispatching the complement of `NO_3^+(8)` computationally should cite the triangular case. In particular, a finite assumption that this host has no EPPA subgraph of half order would be false. The 20 edges of a complete bipartite `K_{4,5}` give an induced `L(K_{4,5})` in `T(9)` of order 36. The manuscript's triangular theorem applies, and complementing both graphs preserves EPPA. This also explains why the parameter tuple alone does not signal a new graph. [Brouwer–Van Maldeghem, Strongly Regular Graphs, §3.1.4](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~aeb/math/srg/rk3/srgw.pdf)
- **The local profiles in `GQ(3,5)`.** The assertion of four isomorphism types for the *entire* local selected graph is stronger than needed. An induced subgraph of exactly nine vertices suffices. In `6K_3`, the degree-profile counts of triples, edges, and singletons with nine total vertices, after excluding the three forbidden induced subgraphs in the preceding sentences, are precisely `(2,1,1)`, `(2,0,3)`, and `(1,1,4)`. The discarded ten-vertex profile `(2,0,4)` has a nine-vertex subset of type `(2,0,3)`. Its full automorphism group would have order `6²·2!·4!=1,728`, rather than `31,104`, but the proof need not count it. The last case uses the automorphism group of the chosen nine vertices. The first two use sections of the pair stabilizer, which is exactly what EPPA supplies. The quotient orders `48` and `144` fail to divide `120`, and `288` fails to divide `2160`.

## Local corrections

- **The ten-vertex orthogonal graph.** The same identification gives the complement of `T(5)` for the complementary orientation at `q=4`. The theorem already includes this graph up to complement.
- **Higman–Sims.** Restore the missing subscript in the bound on the second selected subconstituent.
- **`M_{22}`.** State the argument for any EPPA subset of size at least 39 in that graph, because the Higman–Sims argument applies it to a constituent and requires that scope. The second constituent has order 60 and degree 12. Rank three would give `47μ=12(11−λ)` with `0≤λ≤11`, so `μ=0`. A graph in which distinct nonneighbors have no common neighbor is a union of cliques, and all these cliques would have size 13, which does not divide 60. The rank-three reduction therefore gives the cap of 30 on the selected second constituent. The local independent set of size at least 8 then gives the original contradiction. This proof does not appeal to a classification of smaller EPPA hosts.

## Proof repair for author review

- **Berlekamp–Van Lint–Seidel.** The local cap is on `G*_1`. The host has order 243, so an EPPA subset under consideration has at least 122 vertices. With the root and at most ten local vertices, the second selected constituent has at least 111 vertices, more than half of 220. The nonedge and edge degeneracies are ruled out by its degree 20. Now apply the strict-inequality part of the rank-three reduction to the second subconstituent, which is transitive as a subconstituent of a rank-three graph. Rank three would give the equation `199μ=20(19−λ)`. Since `0≤λ≤19` and `199` and `20` are coprime, this forces `μ=0`. It would therefore be a disjoint union of cliques of order 21, which does not divide 220. This fills the inference in the source, since a non-rank-three graph could in principle be a generalized clique cover.
