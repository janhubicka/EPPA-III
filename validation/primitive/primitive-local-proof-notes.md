## Why the even-even statement needs the EPPA hypothesis

The Paley graph on 29 vertices is connected, transitive and strongly
regular. Its two subconstituents have order 14 and are complementary
isomorphic by multiplication by a nonsquare.

The first is induced on the nonzero squares. At vertex 1, the
nonadjacent vertices with precisely three common neighbours are 16 and
20. The nonadjacent vertex 28 has four common neighbours with 1. The
remaining nonadjacent vertices have two common neighbours with 1.

Multiplication by a square is an automorphism of the first
subconstituent. The squares are generated by 4, and 16 and 20 are
4 squared and 4 to the power −2, respectively. Consequently the
nonadjacent pairs with exactly three common neighbours form two
7-cycles. The automorphism group of the first subconstituent embeds
in the automorphism group of those two cycles, of order
2 times 14 squared. Its group order is prime to 3. By complementation
the same holds for the second subconstituent.

Every graph on at least six vertices has a homogeneous triple. If
either subconstituent were an EPPA witness for such a graph, the
3-cycle on that triple would extend to a graph automorphism whose
order is divisible by 3. This is impossible. Thus both maxima are at
most five. The different nonedge common-neighbour counts also show
that the first subconstituent fails rank three. Rank three is invariant
under complementation, so the second fails it too.
