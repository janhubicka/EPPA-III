# Using permutation-group data in the rank-three catalogue

The graph and automorphism data in a catalogue row can be obtained from a labelled permutation action. A rank-three subgroup in the graph case has three ordered-pair orbits, consisting of the diagonal, the edges, and the distinct nonedges. A permutation in its 2-closure preserves all three. Conversely, every automorphism of the graph sends each ordered pair to one in the same orbit, so some element of the subgroup agrees with it on that pair. Lean proves equality of the 2-closure and the full graph automorphism group as subgroups of the same permutation group.

If a second nontrivial graph is invariant under that subgroup, its adjacency is constant on each of the two off-diagonal orbits. It has both edges and distinct nonedges, so exactly one orbit supplies its edges. This gives the labelled graph isomorphism up to complement. The theorem `finPublishedRankThreeGraphCoverage_of_actions` lets a catalogue supply either this permutation data or the graph isomorphism directly, along with the corresponding `CompleteRankThreeCase` data.

For the square product action, both separate coordinate permutations and transposition preserve the rook relation. Lean derives the rook graph up to complement from a rank-three subgroup contained in the product action. It also derives the same conclusion from invariance under the whole square product action, using the two possible coordinate-equality patterns. The paper invokes the imprimitive product-action classification in §11.2 and the affine instance in Theorem 11.4.1, case (2). [Brouwer and Van Maldeghem, Chapter 11](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~aeb/math/srg/rk3/srgw.pdf)

The affine-line adapter reads a semilinear description of the 2-closure as a statement about every graph automorphism, which is the hypothesis used by the EPPA obstruction. The order adapters use the full group and its rank-three subdegrees to compute point and pair bounds. A published order for a subgroup alone cannot supply these upper bounds. [Skresanov, On 2-closures of rank 3 groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14696)

The small-model bridge recognizes the \((16,6,2,2)\) rook graph from rank three and the strongly regular parameters. In Lean, the nonadjacent-pair orbit forces the local graph to split into two triangles, which determine the two coordinates of the rook graph.

The even ternary exclusion derives the first constituent parameters from the host strongly regular parameters. At the exceptional order of 36, the complementary clique-cover candidate has a pair with at least 15 common neighbors in the first constituent. The host-derived codegree for nonedges of that constituent is nine.

The Gewirtz and \(M_{22}\) adapters derive the needed stabilizer orders from the full graph automorphism orders. The Higman–Sims point stabilizer acts faithfully on the second constituent because the first constituent is independent and its vertices have distinct neighborhoods in the second. The \(M_{22}\) graph identification therefore supplies the Higman–Sims stabilizer bound as well as the second-constituent model. [Brouwer and Van Maldeghem, §§10.20, 10.27, 10.31](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~aeb/math/srg/rk3/srgw.pdf)
