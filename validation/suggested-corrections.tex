\noindent\textbf{Source and policy.}
This is a register of proposed corrections to the manuscript
\emph{Graphs with Small EPPA Witnesses}, referenced against the
user-supplied snapshot \texttt{main-new(7).tex}.
All anchors below use labels present in that source.
The original manuscript is \textbf{frozen}: neither this report nor
passing GitHub Actions authorizes an edit to its TeX.

\paragraph{Evidence levels.}
\textbf{Confirmed}: mathematical counterexample, exact definition
comparison, or unambiguous textual error.
\textbf{Paper-level}: a proposed mathematical proof, not yet
completely certified in Lean.
\textbf{Pending}: a proof dependency or author decision remains.
The Lean CI build certifies only declarations explicitly included
in \texttt{CheckAxioms.lean}; small exhaustive checks do not
establish arbitrary-size results.

\section*{Confirmed localized corrections}

\subsection*{C01. Taylor double of a complete graph}
\textbf{Location:} approximately source lines 522--523, immediately
after Definition~\texttt{def:generalizedcover}.
Definition~\texttt{def:doubles} makes the Taylor double of
\(K_t\) equal to \(2K_t\), with no cross-edges. The connection
type \(M\) adds a matching, and \(\overline M\) is not the
graph complement of that Taylor double.

\textbf{Proposed two-sentence replacement:}
\begin{quote}
For \(G\cong K_t\), the Taylor double is \(2K_t\),
corresponding to connection type \(X=0\).
Connection type \(X=M\) instead gives the Taylor double
with an additional matching between corresponding vertices;
this is another generalised clique cover.
\end{quote}
Keep the next paragraph about transversals of the matching
edges as referring to \(X=M\).

\subsection*{C02. Claim 3.6 and the mate matching}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{claim:taylorconclusion}.
The argument concerns edges between distinct fibres, not
the edge inside each fibre. For
\(G=C_5\dot\cup K_1\), adding all mate edges to the
Taylor double yields a genuine transitive imprimitive EPPA
witness for \(G\) (see the exact finite test
\texttt{checks/section3\_mate\_matching.py}).

\textbf{Replacement statement:}
\begin{quote}
The edges of \(H\) between distinct blocks agree with those
of the Taylor double of \(G\). Within each block, a
uniform matching may additionally be present.
\end{quote}
This makes explicit the existing qualification
\emph{up to complementation within all blocks} in
Lemma~\texttt{lemma:doublecover}.

\subsection*{C07. Lemma 3.12 lacks a hypothesis}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{lemma:different_cliques},
approximately line 1357.

\textbf{Problem (confirmed counterexamples).}
Both of the following satisfy all the written size,
transitivity, imprimitivity and EPPA assumptions:
\[
K_2\dot\cup K_1\subset 3K_2,\qquad
K_3\dot\cup K_2\dot\cup K_1\subset 4K_3.
\]
Yet the first host is neither of the proposed graph types,
and the second has three unequal clique components
rather than \(s=2\). Both witnesses are homogeneous,
which explains why they have EPPA for their induced
subgraphs.

\textbf{Minimal proposed edit:} add the word
\emph{non-homogeneous} before \emph{transitive EPPA witness}
in the lemma hypothesis, matching its use in
Proposition~3.23. An alternative is to state an
additional disjunct allowing homogeneous \(H\).
The first edit is shorter and is the currently proposed
default. Its proof is treated, at paper level, in
\texttt{validation/repair-claims-313-316.tex} and
\texttt{validation/repair-case2-pair-regularity.tex}.
Do not mark the full graph theorem as Lean-certified.

\subsection*{C08. Proposition 3.23 omits disjoint doubles}
\textbf{Location:} alternative \texttt{case:homog} of
\texttt{prop:imprimitive_summary}, approximately
line 1590, and the proof at lines 1610--1612.

\textbf{Problem (confirmed).} The proof erroneously
calls \(2C_5\) and \(2R_3\) homogeneous and removes them.
They are non-homogeneous: a nonedge within one connected
component cannot be interchanged with a nonedge between
components by any automorphism. Nevertheless, both are
transitive imprimitive EPPA witnesses for an embedded
\(G=C_5\), respectively \(G=R_3\), because the single
component \(G\) is itself homogeneous. The main
classification already lists \(H=2G\).

\textbf{Minimal replacement for alternative (4):}
\begin{quote}
\(G\) is homogeneous, and \(H\) is either \(2G\)
or the Taylor double of \(G\).
\end{quote}

\textbf{Matching proof edits:}
replace \emph{Excluding the homogeneous graph \(2C_5\)}
by \emph{The graph \(2C_5\) belongs to Case (4); the
other possibilities are} (adjust the surrounding
sentence), and make the identical correction for \(2R_3\).
Do not delete either witness. The complement case
is handled separately in C09.

\subsection*{C09. Invalid invocation of Lemma 3.3}
\textbf{Location:} the last paragraph of
\texttt{prop:imprimitive_summary},
approximately source lines 1612--1614.

Lemma~\texttt{lemma:doublecover} explicitly excludes
complements of unions of cliques, but is cited there
for \(G=\overline{sK_t}\).

\textbf{Suggested replacement:}
\begin{quote}
For \(G\cong\overline{sK_t}\), complement the EPPA
pair \((G,H)\), apply the equal-clique case to
\((\overline G,\overline H)\), and take complements
again, as permitted in the proposition.
\end{quote}
This requires the complementary-half statement in C10
before the existing conditional Lemma~3.7 can be used.
The added \(2G\) alternative is accounted for by C08.

\subsection*{C04. Claim 3.9: global neighbourhood-profile count}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{claim:connections}.
The sentence asserting that each \(k\)-subset of
\(C_i\) occurs as the neighbourhood of a vertex in a
\emph{fixed} \(D_j\) does not follow from EPPA:
an extending automorphism may move \(D_j\).

\textbf{Replace that sentence by:}
\begin{quote}
Fix \(w\in D_j\) and let \(r\) be the number of
cliques \(C_h\) in which \(w\) has precisely
\(k\) neighbours. Every automorphism of \(G\)
extends to \(H\), so the orbit of the tuple
\((N_H(w)\cap C_h)_{h=1}^{s}\) under
\(\operatorname{Aut}(G)=S_t\wr S_s\) is represented
by vertices of \(H\setminus G\). This orbit has at least
\(\binom{s}{r}\binom{t}{k}^{r}
\ge s\binom{t}{k}\) elements, while
\(|H\setminus G|=st\).
\end{quote}
Keep the existing conclusion \(\binom tk\le t\).
This argument does not need a stabilizer fixing
any individual \(D_j\). It depends on the regularity
already asserted in Claim~3.8.
The author has been reviewing a separate Wagner patch;
do not apply this change to source automatically.

\subsection*{C05. Claim 3.11: Wagner without exhaustive enumeration}
\textbf{Location:} the final exceptional case in the
proof of \texttt{thm:sKt_witnesses}.
The claim that vertex-transitivity alone leaves
one eight-vertex isomorphism type is false.
The finite test in \texttt{checks/cover\_2k2.py}
finds 24 transitive assignments in four types.

\textbf{Preferred repair:}
Reuse C04's orbit estimate to exclude all
off-diagonal matching cases with \(s\ge3\);
the \(s=2,t\ge3\) case reduces to an ordinary
cover by reindexing. For \(s=t=2\),
vertex regularity forces the diagonal entries
to have equal bipartite degree. The degree
zero and two cases also reindex to ordinary
covers. In the remaining case all entries are
matchings; normalize three and write
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}M&M\\M&Z\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad Z\in\{M,\overline M\}.
\]
If \(Z=M\), fixing a vertex of \(C_1\)
and exchanging the two vertices of \(C_2\)
changes the number of common neighbours
from two to zero. Such a partial automorphism
cannot extend to \(H\). Hence \(Z=\overline M\),
giving the Wagner graph.
A source-local TeX paragraph is preserved in
\texttt{validation/short-claim39-wagner.tex}.
The cube obstruction has also been proved in Lean in
\texttt{EPPAIII/CliqueCovers/WagnerReduction.lean}.
This does \emph{not} itself validate the earlier
Claim~3.10 normalization argument.

\subsection*{C10. The missing complementary-half hypothesis}
\textbf{Location:} the use of
\texttt{thm:sKt_witnesses} in the proof of
\texttt{prop:imprimitive_summary}.

\textbf{Problem.} Lemma~3.7 assumes that the
second half of \(H\) is also \(sK_t\).
Proposition~3.23 does not establish that before
invoking it. Equality of the two halves'
edge counts alone would be insufficient.

\textbf{Suggested auxiliary lemma:}
\begin{quote}
Suppose \(H\) is a non-homogeneous
vertex-transitive EPPA witness for an
induced \(G=sK_t\), and \(|H|=2st\).
Then \(H[V(H)\setminus V(G)]\cong sK_t\).
\end{quote}
A self-contained \emph{paper-level} proof using
short \(S_t\wr S_s\)-orbits of neighbourhood profiles
and true-twin classes is given in
\texttt{validation/partition-half-lemma.tex}.
The stronger independent argument in
\texttt{validation/direct-clique-cover-proof.tex}
bypasses Claims~3.8--3.11 entirely but would
constitute a substantial editorial replacement.
Neither graph-level result is yet fully Lean-proved.

\subsection*{C11. Lemma 3.18: a missing bipartite-cycle exclusion}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{lem:C5_10vertex},
the cross-degree-two case (approximately line 1519).

A \(2\)-regular bipartite graph on two parts of size five
could also be \(C_4\dot\cup C_6\);
prime part size does not alone imply \(C_{10}\).

\textbf{Insert the following two sentences:}
\begin{quote}
In the alternative \(C_4\dot\cup C_6\),
the four-cycle singles out a unique pair of
vertices of \(G=C_5\) with two common neighbours
in \(H\setminus G\). Every automorphism of
\(C_5\) extends to \(H\), preserving both halves,
so this pair would be invariant under
\(\operatorname{Aut}(C_5)\), which is impossible.
\end{quote}
Therefore the cross graph is a single \(C_{10}\).
This is a paper-level repair; the rest of the
case classification should be reviewed separately.

\subsection*{C12. Lemma 3.20: correct the cross-degree argument}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{lem:R3_18vertex},
the \(d_{\mathrm{cross}}\in\{2,3,4\}\) cases.

For \(w\in H\setminus G\), the multiset of nine
subsets \(N_H(w)\cap V(G)\) is invariant under
\(\operatorname{Aut}(R_3)\), irrespective of how
the imprimitivity blocks intersect the halves.
The subset orbit sizes are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
k&\text{orbit sizes}\\ \hline
2&18,18\\
3&6,6,36,36\\
4&9,9,36,36,36 .
\end{array}
\]
Thus the first two sizes are impossible,
and a \(4\)-regular cross graph must
correspond to one of the two nine-element
orbits, of neighbourhoods or non-neighbourhoods.
See the full proposed replacement
\texttt{validation/short-r3-cross.tex},
with exact small-orbit checks in
\texttt{checks/section3\_r3\_orbits.py}.
This eliminates an unjustified assumption that the
actions on the two halves have already been
identified equivariantly.
