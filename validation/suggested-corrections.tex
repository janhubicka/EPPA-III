\subsection*{C01. Taylor double of a complete graph}

after Definition~\texttt{def:generalizedcover}.
Definition~\texttt{def:doubles} makes the Taylor double of
\(K_t\) equal to \(2K_t\), with no cross-edges. The connection
type \(M\) adds a matching, and \(\overline M\) is not the
graph complement of that Taylor double.

\textbf{Proposed two-sentence replacement:}
\begin{quote}
For \(G\cong K_t\), the Taylor double is \(2K_t\),
corresponding to connection type \(X=0\).
Connection type \(X=M\) instead gives the Taylor double
with an additional matching between corresponding vertices;
this is another generalised clique cover.
\end{quote}
Keep the next paragraph about transversals of the matching
edges as referring to \(X=M\).

The edges of \(H\) between distinct blocks agree with those

\subsection*{C07. Lemma 3.12 lacks a hypothesis}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{lemma:different\_cliques},
approximately line 1357.

\textbf{Problem (confirmed counterexamples).}

transitivity, imprimitivity and EPPA assumptions:
\[
K_2\dot\cup K_1\subset 3K_2,\qquad
K_3\dot\cup K_2\dot\cup K_1\subset 4K_3.
\]
Yet the first host is neither of the proposed graph types,
and the second has three unequal clique components

\textbf{Minimal proposed edit:} add the word
\emph{non-homogeneous} before \emph{transitive EPPA witness}

Proposition~3.23. An alternative is to state an

The first edit is shorter and is the currently proposed

\subsection*{C04. Claim 3.9: global neighbourhood-profile count}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{claim:connections}.
The sentence asserting that each \(k\)-subset of
\(C_i\) occurs as the neighbourhood of a vertex in a
\emph{fixed} \(D_j\) does not follow from EPPA:
an extending automorphism may move \(D_j\).

\textbf{Replace that sentence by:}
\begin{quote}
Fix \(w\in D_j\) and let \(r\) be the number of
cliques \(C_h\) in which \(w\) has precisely
\(k\) neighbours. Every automorphism of \(G\)
extends to \(H\), so the orbit of the tuple
\((N_H(w)\cap C_h)_{h=1}^{s}\) under
\(\operatorname{Aut}(G)=S_t\wr S_s\) is represented
by vertices of \(H\setminus G\). This orbit has at least
\(\binom{s}{r}\binom{t}{k}^{r}
\ge s\binom{t}{k}\) elements, while
\(|H\setminus G|=st\).
\end{quote}
Keep the existing conclusion \(\binom tk\le t\).
The profile orbit estimate applies \emph{without}
Claim~3.8's two-sided regularity and does not need
a stabilizer fixing any particular \(D_j\).
However, Claim~3.8 (or a replacement for it) is still
needed to infer that each whole \(C_i\)--\(D_j\)
bipartite graph is a matching or co-matching, rather
than merely that each vertex has an extreme degree.

\paragraph{Formalization evidence.}
The global EPPA orbit upper bound is Lean-verified in
\texttt{EPPAIII/EPPA/ProfileOrbitBound.lean}.
The numerical estimate
\(\binom{s}{r}\binom{t}{k}^{r}
\ge s\binom{t}{k}\), including the case \(r=s\),
and the resulting restriction to \(k=1,t-1\)
are implemented in
\texttt{EPPAIII/CliqueCovers/Claim39Arithmetic.lean}.
An independent generator-based exhaustive orbit check
for \(st\le12\) is in
\texttt{checks/section3\_wreath\_orbits.py}.
The \emph{general} wreath-product orbit lower bound
\(\binom{s}{r}\binom{t}{k}^{r}\le
|\operatorname{Aut}(G)\cdot P(w)|\) is now Lean-verified
in \texttt{EPPAIII/CliqueCovers/WreathOrbitCount.lean}.
The orbit-to-EPPA bridge and the exact \(|H|=2st\)
cardinality specialization have now been Lean-verified
and merged as PR~\#29 in
\texttt{EPPAIII/CliqueCovers/Claim39OrbitBridge.lean}.
In particular, the audited theorem
\texttt{eppa\_clique\_degree\_extreme\_of\_double}
proves the Claim~3.9 extreme-degree restriction
\emph{without} Claim~3.8 or stabilization of a \(D_j\).
The two-sided regularity for complete bipartite blocks
is still an independent open proof issue (C14);
\texttt{validation/short-mixed-partial-orbits.tex}
records an alternative avoiding this dependency.
The author has been reviewing a separate Wagner patch;
do not apply this change to source automatically.

\subsection*{C05. Claim 3.11: Wagner without exhaustive enumeration}
\textbf{Location:} the final exceptional case in the
proof of \texttt{thm:sKt\_witnesses}.
The claim that vertex-transitivity alone leaves
one eight-vertex isomorphism type is false.
The finite test in \texttt{checks/cover\_2k2.py}
finds 24 transitive assignments in four types.

\textbf{Preferred repair:}
Reuse C04's orbit estimate to exclude all
off-diagonal matching cases with \(s\ge3\);
the \(s=2,t\ge3\) case reduces to an ordinary
cover by reindexing. For \(s=t=2\),
vertex regularity forces the diagonal entries
to have equal bipartite degree. The degree
zero and two cases also reindex to ordinary
covers. In the remaining case all entries are
matchings; normalize three and write
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}M&M\\M&Z\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad Z\in\{M,\overline M\}.
\]
If \(Z=M\), fixing a vertex of \(C_1\)
and exchanging the two vertices of \(C_2\)
changes the number of common neighbours
from two to zero. Such a partial automorphism
cannot extend to \(H\). Hence \(Z=\overline M\),
giving the Wagner graph.
A source-local TeX paragraph is preserved in
\texttt{validation/short-claim39-wagner.tex}.
The cube obstruction has also been proved in Lean in
\texttt{EPPAIII/CliqueCovers/WagnerReduction.lean}.
This does \emph{not} itself validate the earlier
Claim~3.10 normalization argument.

\subsection*{C11. Lemma 3.18: a missing bipartite-cycle exclusion}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{lem:C5\_10vertex},
the cross-degree-two case (approximately line 1519).

A \(2\)-regular bipartite graph on two parts of size five
could also be \(C_4\dot\cup C_6\);
prime part size does not alone imply \(C_{10}\).

\textbf{Insert the following two sentences:}
\begin{quote}
In the alternative \(C_4\dot\cup C_6\),
the four-cycle singles out a unique pair of
vertices of \(G=C_5\) with two common neighbours
in \(H\setminus G\). Every automorphism of
\(C_5\) extends to \(H\), preserving both halves,
so this pair would be invariant under
\(\operatorname{Aut}(C_5)\), which is impossible.
\end{quote}
Therefore the cross graph is a single \(C_{10}\).
This is a paper-level repair; the rest of the
case classification should be reviewed separately.

\subsection*{C12. Lemma 3.20: correct the cross-degree argument}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{lem:R3\_18vertex},
the \(d_{\mathrm{cross}}\in\{2,3,4\}\) cases.

For \(w\in H\setminus G\), the multiset of nine
subsets \(N_H(w)\cap V(G)\) is invariant under
\(\operatorname{Aut}(R_3)\), irrespective of how
the imprimitivity blocks intersect the halves.
The subset orbit sizes are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
k&\text{orbit sizes}\\ \hline
2&18,18\\
3&6,6,36,36\\
4&9,9,36,36,36 .
\end{array}
\]
Thus the first two sizes are impossible,
and a \(4\)-regular cross graph must
correspond to one of the two nine-element
orbits, of neighbourhoods or non-neighbourhoods.
See the full proposed replacement
\texttt{validation/short-r3-cross.tex},
with exact small-orbit checks in
\texttt{checks/section3\_r3\_orbits.py}.
This eliminates an unjustified assumption that the
actions on the two halves have already been
identified equivariantly.

\subsection*{C16. Claim 3.4: explain why a whole block is an EPPA witness}
\textbf{Location:} the first unnumbered claim in
Lemma~\texttt{lemma:doublecover}, approximately
lines 952--955 of \texttt{main-new(7).tex}.

The draft argues that if both edges and nonedges of
\(G\) lie within blocks, then one block \(\Pi\)
is itself an EPPA witness for \(G\).
This is correct but requires the observation that
every nonempty partial-automorphism extension stabilizes
\(\Pi\) setwise. We found no counterexample to this claim.

\textbf{Suggested one-sentence insertion:}
\begin{quote}
Indeed, \(\Pi\) contains \(V(G)\) and
\(|\Pi|\le n\), so \(\Pi=V(G)\);
an extension of a nonempty partial automorphism
of \(G\) stabilizes \(\Pi\), hence restricts
to an automorphism of \(G\).
\end{quote}

An independent proof audit is in
\texttt{validation/claim34-block-system.tex}.
Lean now verifies the block-orbit step,
cluster-obstruction reduction, whole-block
homogeneity contradiction, the pair-count
conclusion \emph{from vertex-transitivity},
and construction of the canonical equivalence
\(\{0,1\}\times V(G)\simeq V(H)\).
The transport theorems preserve both EPPA
and the invariant pair system, allowing the
structural Taylor-double conclusion with
uniform optional mate edges.

\texttt{BlockSystems/FullDoubleCover.lean},

\subsection*{C17. Lemma 3.3: optional short proof of inter-block orbit transitivity}
\textbf{Location:} the paragraph immediately after
Claim~\texttt{claim:taylorconclusion} in
Lemma~\texttt{lemma:doublecover}, approximately
lines 1030--1035 of \texttt{main-new(7).tex}.

\textbf{Status: optional proof simplification, not a
counterexample.} The source derives transitivity
of ordered inter-block edges and nonedges from
the more elaborate Claim~\texttt{claim:orbit}.
There is a direct short argument once the Taylor
inter-fibre pattern is available.

\textbf{Suggested replacement paragraph:}
\begin{quote}
Let \(K\) denote the permutation group induced by
\(\operatorname{Aut}(H)\) on the two-vertex blocks.
By two-point EPPA, \(K\) is transitive on ordered
pairs corresponding to edges of \(G\), and separately
on those corresponding to its nonedges. If these
orbits were distinct, every induced permutation
would preserve \(G\), and EPPA would make \(G\)
homogeneous, contrary to our assumption.
Thus \(K\) is 2-transitive.
In the Taylor double, each ordered pair of
distinct blocks supports precisely two
ordered edges and two ordered nonedges.
The deck involution that exchanges the
vertices of every block interchanges the
two choices of each type.
Therefore \(\operatorname{Aut}(H)\) is
transitive on ordered inter-block edges
and, separately, on ordered inter-block
nonedges.
\end{quote}

The complete source-local argument is in
\texttt{validation/short-lemma33-orbits.tex}.
Its essential graph and group-action steps
are now Lean-verified in
\texttt{EPPAIII/DoubleCover/FibreOrbits.lean},
\texttt{EPPAIII/DoubleCover/DeckSwap.lean},
\texttt{EPPAIII/DoubleCover/InterblockOrbits.lean},
and \texttt{EPPAIII/BlockSystems/OrderedInterblockOrbits.lean}.
As elsewhere, the connection between the source
lemma's list of excluded homogeneous graphs and
the Lean non-homogeneity hypothesis uses the
Gardiner classification at paper level.
The original manuscript remains frozen;
the paragraph above is an author-review option.


\subsection*{C18. Claim 3.9: explicitly justify the canonical outside labels}
\textbf{Location:} after the neighbourhood-profile orbit estimate in
Claim~\texttt{claim:connections}, when it is used for the
one-partial-coordinate case of Lemma~3.7.

\textbf{Status:} a source-local clarification, not a counterexample.
Assume \(s,t\ge2\) and an EPPA witness \(H\) on \(2st\) vertices
containing the induced \(sK_t\), and let \(w\) be an outside vertex.
If a size-\(k\) coordinate occurs in \(r\) places and
\[
     \binom sr\binom tk^{\,r}=st,
\]
then the \(S_t\wr S_s\) orbit of \(P(w)\) saturates the entire
outside-vertex budget. The profile map on the outside half is
therefore injective, and every permitted assignment of \(r\)
indices and their \(k\)-subsets is realised by exactly one
outside vertex. This conclusion does \emph{not} need a coherent
choice of EPPA automorphism extensions.

\textbf{Suggested insertion (if retaining the original proof):}
\begin{quote}
In the equality case the orbit bound is saturated.
Consequently every vertex outside \(G\) has a distinct
neighbourhood in \(G\), and the outside vertices are
canonically indexed by the corresponding wreath-orbit
choices. Any extension of a base automorphism has
the same action on these outside vertices.
\end{quote}

The injection and unique realisation are Lean-verified
in \texttt{CliqueCovers/OutsideProfileBijection.lean} and
\texttt{CliqueCovers/OutsideChoiceEquivalence.lean}
(PR~\#90, merged with green CI).
The exact equality between the finite image of the choice
extraction map and the full combinatorial choice space
is Lean-verified in \texttt{CliqueCovers/OutsideChoiceImage.lean}
(PR~\#91, merged with green CI). A canonical equivalence of
the outside-vertex type and the choice subtype is additionally
verified in \texttt{CliqueCovers/OutsideChoiceEquiv.lean}
(PR~\#92, merged with green CI).
The subsequent singleton and co-singleton identification of this
choice space with \(\operatorname{Fin}(s)\times\operatorname{Fin}(t)\),
and transport of the outside relation under genuine EPPA lifts,
were verified in the one-partial host classification
(PR~\#128, merged green).
The separate Wagner reduction in C05 is unaffected.


\paragraph{Formalization status note (12 October 2026).}
For \(s,t\ge2\), the natural wreath action on
\(\operatorname{Fin}(s)\times\operatorname{Fin}(t)\) has only the
four invariant simple graphs
\(\emptyset,\ sK_t,\ \overline{sK_t},\ K_{st}\).
The degree computations for these four cases and the strict
comparisons with \(t-1\) are Lean-verified and merged
in \texttt{CliqueCovers/CliqueUnionDegrees.lean}
(PR~\#94). The purely graph-theoretic deduction
\[
  B\text{ is wreath-invariant},\qquad
  |E(B)|=|E(sK_t)|
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad B=sK_t
\]
is Lean-verified in
\texttt{CliqueCovers/WreathEqualEdgeRigidity.lean}
(PR~\#95, merged with green CI). An equivariant abstract
labelling of the actual outside graph is enough to apply
this deduction by the Lean-verified
\texttt{CliqueCovers/EquivariantWreathAssembly.lean}
