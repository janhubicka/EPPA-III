\paragraph{S3-01, Lemma 3.12: false without non-homogeneous witness.}
Take \(G=K_2\dot\cup K_1\subset H=3K_2\). Then \(H\) is homogeneous,
transitive and imprimitive, and \(2|G|=|H|\), but is not one of
the graphs in the conclusion. More strongly
\(K_3\dot\cup K_2\dot\cup K_1\subset 4K_3\) contradicts \(s=2\).
Add the hypothesis that \(H\) is non-homogeneous; this is necessary,
not yet a complete repair of the proof.
\paragraph{S3-02, Proposition 3.23: the doubles are missing.}
Both \(2C_5\) and \(2R_3\) are non-homogeneous, transitive and imprimitive,
and each is an EPPA witness for one embedded \(C_5\) or \(R_3\).
They are incorrectly called homogeneous in the proof.
Case (4) should include \(H=2G\), as does the main theorem.
\paragraph{S3-03, Proposition 3.23: invalid invocation of Lemma 3.3.}
The final paragraph invokes Lemma 3.3 for complements of
unions of cliques, which that lemma explicitly excludes.
Complement both \(G\) and \(H\) and use the equal-clique
case, after settling the partition hypothesis below.
\paragraph{S3-04, Proposition 3.23: missing partition hypothesis.}
Lemma 3.7 assumes not only that \(G\cong sK_t\) but also that
\(H\setminus G\cong sK_t\). Proposition 3.23 invokes Lemma 3.7
without first showing the latter property. Regularity and
\(|G|=|H|/2\) give equal edge counts in the two halves,
not isomorphism. The \(s=t=2\) instance is checked separately;
the general transfer remains open.
\paragraph{S3-05, Claim 3.15: structural inference needs repair.}
An edge between different blocks of imprimitivity need not
automatically join two blocks meeting \(G\). Also extending
permutations of \(G\cap\Pi\) while fixing a vertex of \(\Pi'\)
does not by itself establish a faithful action on
\(\Pi'\setminus G\). Hence the claimed inequality
\(|\Pi'\setminus G|\geq |G\cap\Pi|\) requires an argument,
as do the subsequent equality and two-block conclusions.
This is a proof gap, not a known counterexample when \(H\)
is assumed non-homogeneous.
\paragraph{S3-06, Claims 3.13 and 3.16: block action details.}
In Claim 3.13, the assertion that each block meets at most
one maximal clique of \(G\) can be justified by selecting an
independent transversal of the maximal cliques and extending all
its permutations. The invariant block-equivalence relation must
be constant on unordered pairs of that transversal; all points
cannot occupy one block since \(G\) does not. In Claim 3.16,
the preservation of selected \(D_i\)'s and the inclusion
\(\varphi(C_1\setminus K)\subseteq D_1\) need justification.
\paragraph{S3-07, Lemma 3.18: one-sentence repair of the cross-degree 2 case.}
A 2-regular bipartite graph on \(5+5\) vertices is either
\(C_{10}\) or \(C_4\dot\cup C_6\).
In the second case its \(C_4\) determines the unique pair of
vertices of \(G=C_5\) having two common neighbours in
\(H\setminus G\). Every automorphism of \(C_5\) extends to \(H\)
and preserves both halves, so this pair would be invariant
under \(\operatorname{Aut}(C_5)\). But its edge and nonedge
orbits on unordered pairs both have size five.
Thus the bipartite cross graph is \(C_{10}\).
This replaces the unsupported claim that prime size alone
forces one cycle.
\paragraph{S3-09, Lemma 3.20: the two-block action gap is now
repaired at paper level (Lean pending).}
The proof need not identify the two actions first. For
\(w\in V'=H\setminus G\), set \(S_w=N_H(w)\cap V(G)\).
Since every automorphism of \(G=R_3\) extends to one of \(H\)
preserving \(G\) and \(V'\), the multiset
\((S_w:w\in V')\) is invariant under
\(A=\operatorname{Aut}(R_3)\).
Each set has cardinality \(d_{\rm cross}\), and there are
nine sets counted with multiplicities. Directly classifying
the orbits of \(A\) on subsets gives the lists
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
k&\text{orbit sizes on }k\text{-subsets}\\ \hline
2 &18,18\\
3 &6,6,36,36\\
4 &9,9,36,36,36
\end{array}
\]
The lists exclude \(d_{\rm cross}=2,3\), since no invariant
multiset of total size nine can be built from the listed
orbits. When \(d_{\rm cross}=4\), the nine sets must be
precisely one of the two size-nine orbits: all neighbourhoods
of vertices of \(R_3\), or all non-neighbourhoods.
The induced equivariant correspondence \(w\mapsto S_w\)
forces the second half to carry either the natural
\(R_3\) adjacency or its complement. If its adjacency is
the complement under this correspondence, vertices in the
two halves belong to respectively 10 and 12 triangles.
That contradicts vertex-transitivity. The two surviving
possibilities are exactly the cross neighbour and
cross non-neighbour constructions in the manuscript.

This short argument works \emph{for both types of block
system}, without assuming a matching beforehand.
The orbit and triangle calculations have reproducible
certificates in \texttt{checks/section3\_r3\_orbits.py}.
They are paper- and Python-verified, \emph{not Lean proved}.

\paragraph{S3-08, finite validation for the \(2K_2\) complement.}
Suppose \(H\) is transitive on eight vertices and is EPPA
for an induced \(G=2K_2\) occupying four vertices.
Since \(H\) is regular and the halves have equal size,
the two induced halves have the same number of edges.
Thus \(H\setminus G\) has precisely two edges:
\(2K_2\) or \(P_3\dot\cup K_1\).
For the second possibility a standard-library exhaustive
check finds 864 cross-edge matrices compatible with regularity,
96 vertex-transitive ones, and \emph{zero} EPPA witnesses
for the designated \(2K_2\). Therefore \(H\setminus G
\cong2K_2\) in this finite case, without assuming it.
See \texttt{checks/section3\_2k2\_complement.py}.
This does not establish the corresponding general statement
for arbitrary \(s,t\).

\section*{Further repairs of Claims 3.13--3.16}
\paragraph{S3--05, Claim 3.15 (paper-level repair; Lean pending).}
A vertex-transitive, positive-degree block quotient and
independent transversals of the unequal cliques show that
every imprimitivity block meets \(G\).
EPPA then makes the degree into each other block a
constant \(r>0\); double-counting the cross edges gives
\(k_ir=(m-k_j)r\) for every distinct \(i,j\), where
\(k_i=|G\cap\Pi_i|\). Thus \(k_i+k_j=m\), forcing
precisely two blocks if the \(k_i\)'s are unequal.
The detailed rigorous proof is in
\texttt{validation/repair-claims-313-316.tex}.

\paragraph{Case 1: the cross relation is a matching.}
For two blocks the interblock-edge graph splits into
\(r\)-regular bipartite graphs on \(2k_1\) and \(2k_2\)
vertices. If \(r\ge2\), EPPA and the shared-neighbour
relation force both pieces to be connected. This violates
vertex-transitivity since \(k_1\ne k_2\).
Hence \(r=1\). The case \(r=0\) is homogeneous
and must be handled separately.

\paragraph{S3--06, Claim 3.16 (paper-level repair; Lean arithmetic checked).}
For the Taylor double of \(\dot\cup_iK_{k_i}\), with or
without the extra fibre matching, the triangle count
at a vertex above the \(i\)-th clique is
\[
 \sum_j\binom{k_j}{2}+(k_i-1)(n-k_i-1).
\]
The difference for indices \(i,j\) equals
\((k_i-k_j)(n-k_i-k_j)\). It cannot vanish
for unequal \(k_i,k_j\) when \(s\ge3\).
The formula passed 2,024 exhaustive finite graph tests;
its algebraic difference is verified by Lean, but the
graph-counting statement itself is not yet formalized.

\section*{S3--04: complementary-half partition now proved at paper level}
The missing hypothesis in the application of Lemma 3.7
is supplied by a general lemma:
if \(H\) is a non-homogeneous vertex-transitive
EPPA witness for an induced \(G=sK_t\) with
\(|H|=2st\), then \(H\setminus G\cong sK_t\).
It does not require an imprimitivity assumption.

For each vertex \(w\) outside \(G\), record the
\(s\)-tuple \((N(w)\cap C_i)\).
Its orbit under \(S_t\wr S_s\) is realised
by vertices outside \(G\), so has size \(\le st\).
The exact orbit formula forces either all coordinates
empty/full, or a unique partial coordinate which is a
singleton or co-singleton, with all remaining coordinates
uniformly empty/full. The latter profiles form the
natural \(st\)-point wreath-product action.
In the only extra small case \(s=t=2\), profiles with
one point from each clique give the square action.
The induced outside graph is determined in each case
by its edge count, which equals that of \(G\).

If instead all profiles are unions of complete cliques,
each \(C_i\) is a true-twin class subset in \(H\).
Vertex-transitivity and the independent-set bound in the
regular true-twin quotient force exactly \(2s\) twin
classes of size \(t\); the selected \(s\) classes
are exactly \(G\), and the other \(s\) form \(sK_t\)
by the same edge count.

The complete self-contained proof, including the
trivial \(s=1\) and \(t=1\) cases, is in
\texttt{validation/partition-half-lemma.tex}.
The requisite small orbit calculations were independently
checked for 52,368 profiles in
\texttt{checks/section3\_profiles.py}.
This repairs the missing hypothesis transfer in
Proposition 3.23, provided \(H\) is non-homogeneous.
For complementary \(G\), apply the lemma to
\((\overline G,\overline H)\), not Lemma 3.3.
The general lemma is not yet formalized in Lean.

\section*{Case 2 of Lemma 3.12: pair-system structure repaired}
The earlier appeal to Claims 3.5--3.6 can be
replaced by two-point EPPA and vertex regularity.
Once the invariant blocks are pairs
\(\{x_0,x_1\}\), with \(G\) selecting one
point per block, all ordered edges of \(G\)
lie in one \(\operatorname{Aut}(H)\)-orbit, as
do all ordered nonedges. Therefore the
cross-layer adjacency bits \(c_E,c_N\)
and top-layer bits \(e_T,n_T\) depend only
on whether \(x,y\) are adjacent in \(G\);
the within-fibre bit \(\varepsilon\) is constant
by transitivity.
For a vertex over a \(G\)-clique of size \(k\),
regularity gives
\[
d_0(k)=k-1+\varepsilon+c_E(k-1)+c_N(n-k),
\]
whose coefficient of the varying \(k\)
must vanish. Thus \(c_E=0,c_N=1\).
Comparing top degrees
\[
d_1(k)=\varepsilon+e_T(k-1)+n_T(n-k)+(n-k)
\]
with \(d_0(k)=n-1+\varepsilon\)
forces \(e_T=1,n_T=0\).
This is exactly the Taylor double with
optional matching between fibre mates.
Claim 3.16 is then settled by the
triangle count already recorded.
The exact local manuscript replacement is
\texttt{validation/repair-case2-pair-regularity.tex}.
All 7,520 invariant binary-matrix patterns
for unequal compositions of base orders
\(3\le n\le8\) pass the independent
degree calculation in
\texttt{checks/section3\_pair\_regular.py}.
