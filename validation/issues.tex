\section*{Issues}

\paragraph{GCC--01 (confirmed): Taylor-double identification.}
The passage following Definition 1.6 says that, for \(G=K_t\),
diagonal connection \(X=M\) gives exactly the Taylor double of \(K_t\).
Definition 2.2 instead gives \(2K_t\), so the Taylor double corresponds
to \(X=0\). The matching connection has \(t\) additional cross-edges.
The analogous complement wording should also be revised.
Lean target: \texttt{matching\_not\_taylor\_clique}.

\paragraph{GCC--02 (proof repair required): Claim 3.8.}
The argument with coloured cycles needs a canonical connection profile,
not a profile dependent on arbitrary orderings of vertices inside the
\(D_j\)'s. One possible repair is to use, for every \(D_j\), the vector
whose \(i\)-th coordinate is the multiset of neighbourhoods of the
vertices of \(D_j\) in \(C_i\). If an automorphism extending a
transposition in \(C_i\) maps \(D_j\) to \(D_{j'}\), the coordinates other
than \(i\) agree. Unequal degrees of the transposed vertices force
the \(i\)-th coordinates to differ. Then the no-rainbow-cycle argument
must be stated for these actual profiles. This is a proposed repair
and has not been checked in Lean.

\paragraph{GCC--03 (repaired at paper level; Lean pending): Claim 3.9.}
The manuscript argues incorrectly that every \(k\)-subset of \(C_i\)
occurs as the neighbourhood of a vertex of a \emph{fixed} \(D_j\).
Extensions may move the \(D_j\)'s. There is, however, a stronger
orbit-counting argument using \emph{all} vertices of \(H\setminus G\).

Fix \(w\in H\setminus G\) and put
\(P(w)=(N_H(w)\cap C_1,\ldots,N_H(w)\cap C_s)\).
Every element of \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)=S_t\wr S_s\) extends to
an automorphism of \(H\) which preserves \(G\) and \(H\setminus G\).
It follows that the entire wreath-product orbit of \(P(w)\) is
realised by profiles of the \(st\) vertices of \(H\setminus G\).

Suppose one coordinate of \(P(w)\) has size \(k\), with
\(1\leq k\leq t-1\), and let \(r\geq1\) be the number of coordinates
having size \(k\). The action can choose the \(r\) positions among
the \(s\) cliques and, independently, realise all
\(\binom tk\) subsets in each selected coordinate. Consequently
the orbit has size at least
\[
  \binom sr \binom tk^r \ \geq\ s\binom tk.
\]
The last inequality holds for \(r=1\) by equality; for
\(1<r<s\) use \(\binom sr\geq s\);
and for \(r=s\) use \(B^{s-1}\geq s\) for
\(B=\binom tk\geq2\). Since the orbit fits in \(st\)
available vertices, \(\binom tk\leq t\), which forces
\(k\in\{1,t-1\}\).

Assuming Claim 3.8 establishes that the bipartite block is
\(k\)-regular on both sides, apply this argument to any
\(w\in D_j\) to prove precisely the four connection possibilities
of Claim 3.9. The orbit proof itself requires no stabilisation
of \(D_j\). The proof is mathematically complete at this level,
but is \textbf{not yet Lean-checked}. The missing two-sided
regularity in Claim 3.8 remains a separate dependency.

\paragraph{GCC--04 (gap): Claim 3.10.}
The colour-count argument on the clique-adjacency graph requires a
precisely identified action on the \(D_j\)'s. Its assertion of a
common number \(k_m\) of colour-\(m\) neighbours for every \(D_j\)
does not follow from transitivity on the \(C_i\)'s alone.
Even when all bipartite entries are isomorphic matchings,
independently reordering each \(D_j\) to turn every entry into
the same literal matching requires compatibility of the relative
permutations. A better strategy is to rule out off-diagonal
matching cases first and then normalise only the diagonal matchings.

\paragraph{GCC--05 (confirmed flaw): Claim 3.11 when \(s\geq3,t=2\).}
The calculation of \(N(I)\cap(H\setminus G)\) omits diagonal
connections. In particular, if \(X=1\), a transversal \(I\)
choosing one vertex of each \(C_j\) is adjacent to every vertex
of \(D_j\), for every \(j\). Hence
\(N(I)\cap(H\setminus G)=H\setminus G\) independently of \(I\).
The proof's claimed variation of this neighbourhood size therefore
fails without first excluding \(X=1\). This is an objection to
the \emph{argument}, not a counterexample to Lemma 3.7.
Lean target: \texttt{full\_diag\_hits\_every\_right}.

\paragraph{GCC--06 (gaps): remaining cases of Claim 3.11.}
In the \(s\geq3,t\geq3\) case, an extension fixing \(C_1\)
pointwise may permute the \(D_j\)'s; the proof assumes \(D_3\)
is fixed setwise. In the \(s=2,t\geq3\) case, the sentence
claiming that \emph{both} diagonal and off-diagonal entries
are matchings is not a consequence of the preceding claim:
the diagonal entries may still be \(0\) or \(1\).
These possibilities must be handled.

\paragraph{GCC--07 (short hand repair identified): Claim 3.11.}
The claim that the Hadamard/Wagner pattern is the only
\emph{transitive} graph in the final \(s=t=2\) case is false.
Enumerating the 64 choices of diagonal entries from
\(\{0,1,M,\overline M\}\) and off-diagonal entries from
\(\{M,\overline M\}\) yields 24 transitive matrices in four
isomorphism classes. Exactly 16 matrices are EPPA witnesses
for the embedded \(2K_2\). The classes are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
Representative & Transitive matrices & EPPA matrices & Edges\\
\midrule
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&M\\M&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) &4&4&8\\
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&M\\M&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) &4&4&16\\
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}M&M\\M&M\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) &8&0&12\\
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}M&M\\\overline M&M\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) &8&8&12\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The first two are ordinary covers after reindexing, the third
is the cube (fails EPPA for the designated copy), and the fourth
is the Wagner graph. The finite enumeration is no longer needed
in the manuscript: equal vertex degrees force the two diagonal
connection types to have the same bipartite degree. If that
degree is (0) or (2), swap (D_1,D_2) to obtain a uniform cover.
Otherwise all four entries are matchings; after independent
relabeling, three are (M), leaving (Zin{M,overline M}).
For (Z=M), a fixed vertex in (C_1) has two common neighbours
with one vertex of (C_2), and none with its mate; swapping these
two vertices in (G) violates EPPA. Thus (Z=overline M),
the Wagner graph. The cube obstruction is separately proved
in Lean as 	exttt{EPPAIII.cubeMatching_not_EPPA}. The reproducible exhaustive check is
\texttt{checks/cover\_2k2.py}, written using Python's standard library.

\paragraph{GCC--08 (confirmed full finite test): the whole \(s=t=2\) case.}
The 64 special matrices above are only a subset of the possible
cross-relations. We independently enumerated all \(2^{16}=65{,}536\)
bipartite graphs between the two distinguished copies of \(2K_2\),
without imposing any regularity on the four \(2\times2\) cross-blocks.
Vertex transitivity implies uniform total degree, reducing the
candidates to 140 cross matrices. Exactly 68 of these are
vertex-transitive, and 28 have EPPA for the designated \(2K_2\).
They represent six graph-isomorphism types.

Crucially, the test does \emph{not} stop at graph isomorphism:
for each of the 28 accepted matrices, it enumerates all
independent rearrangements of the cliques and vertices
on the \(C\) and \(D\) sides (the action of
\(\operatorname{Aut}(2K_2)\times\operatorname{Aut}(2K_2)\)).
Every accepted matrix is equivalent under these \emph{permitted}
relabellings to a uniform generalised clique cover
(Definition 1.6) or to the Wagner matrix. Hence the exact
finite \(s=t=2\) instance of Lemma 3.7, under its partition
hypotheses, is independently verified. This is exhaustive
Python verification, not Lean proof.
Reproduction: \texttt{checks/cover\_2k2\_all.py}.

\paragraph{GCC--09 (minor statement correction): graph complements.}
The displayed exceptional connection matrix has all four
cross-blocks of matching type, so every vertex has degree \(3\).
The complement of the Wagner graph has degree \(4\).
Consequently, the phrase \emph{or its complement} in the matrix
case of Lemma~3.7 is not literally supported by that matrix:
the complementary classification is obtained by complementing
the entire pair \((G,H)\). Likewise, the assignment obtained
by replacing each matching with its complementary matching
is \emph{isomorphic} to the Wagner graph (the parity of the
four matchings is unchanged); it is not the complement.

\section*{Further Section 3 validation: unequal clique sizes}
\textbf{S3--05} (Claim 3.15) is now repaired at paper level:
the block quotient is complete; all blocks are occupied;
a uniform positive number \(r\) of neighbours in every
other block gives \(k_ir=(m-k_j)r\).
Unequal clique sizes force exactly two blocks.
The cross-edge graph then decomposes into pieces on
\(2k_1,2k_2\) vertices; EPPA and vertex-transitivity
exclude \(r>1\), leaving a perfect matching.

\textbf{S3--06} (Claim 3.16) admits a triangle-count
repair: the difference between vertices above cliques
\(i,j\) in the Taylor double is
\((k_i-k_j)(n-k_i-k_j)\), unchanged by adding
the matching between fibre mates. For at least
three cliques with unequal sizes this contradicts
vertex-transitivity. The formula passed 2,024
exact finite double-graph checks and the polynomial
difference is Lean-verified.

See \texttt{validation/repair-claims-313-316.tex}
for the complete hypotheses and proof details.
\emph{Remaining:} add the non-homogeneous hypothesis
to Lemma 3.12, close its Case 2 structural dependency,
and justify the second-half partition before applying
Lemma 3.7 in Proposition 3.23.

\section*{Closed paper-level gap: complementary half of \(sK_t\)}
\textbf{S3--04} (application of Lemma 3.7 in
Proposition 3.23) now has an independent proof:
for every \emph{non-homogeneous, vertex-transitive}
EPPA witness \(H\) for \(G=sK_t\) with
\(|H|=2st\), the induced graph on \(H\setminus G\)
is also \(sK_t\). The proof distinguishes short
wreath-product orbits of outside neighbourhood
profiles from the true-twin case. It works without
imprimitivity; the full argument is in
\texttt{validation/partition-half-lemma.tex}.
A checker confirms the orbit classification in
52,368 finite cases. The graph-theoretic lemma
itself still awaits Lean formalization.

\textbf{S3--03} (complementary \(G\)) is then handled
by complementing both \(G\) and \(H\) before applying
the new lemma and Lemma 3.7. Lemma 3.3 is not
used outside its hypotheses.
